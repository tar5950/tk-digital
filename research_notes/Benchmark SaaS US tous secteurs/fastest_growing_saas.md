# Fastest-Growing SaaS in the US (2024-2026): AI-native, AI voice for SMBs, vertical SMB SaaS, SMB HR/finance

Method note: research date 2026-09-29. Most primary pages (TechCrunch, Sacra, SEC, ElevenLabs blog, trade press) were blocked by the network proxy for full-text fetch, so many figures below come from search-result snippets of those sources. Where the only source is an aggregator (getlatka, valueaddvc, newmarketpitch, analyze-ai, clinkbill, etc.), this is flagged as [AGGREGATOR]. Company-reported ARR is always a vendor claim unless audited (public filings); flagged [VENDOR CLAIM] where it matters. "ARR" in AI companies is often annualized run-rate of the latest month, including usage revenue, not contracted recurring revenue.

## 1. ARR, growth speed (time to $100M ARR), valuation and pricing per company

### Takeaway
AI-native apps have compressed "time to $100M ARR" from 5-10 years (classic SaaS) to 8-24 months: Lovable (~8 months), Cursor (~12 months from $1M; ~21 months from launch), Emergent (~8 months), Legora (<18 months), ElevenLabs and Sierra (~20 months). Cursor then went to $2B (Feb 2026) and >$4B (Jun 2026) run-rate. Scaled SMB platforms (Gusto, Rippling, Deel, Ramp, ServiceTitan) grow 15-100% on a $0.8-1.5B base; Ramp is the standout (~$1.5B run-rate, $44B valuation).

## 2. Which categories sell best to local/SMB businesses (reviews, messaging, booking, AI receptionist, payments, payroll)

### Takeaway
The scaled SMB winners monetize core "money flows" — payroll (Gusto ~$1B), spend/cards (Ramp ~$1.5B), and payments embedded in vertical systems of record (ServiceTitan $82B GTV, GlossGenius). Messaging/reviews (Podium, Birdeye, Weave) are $100-250M businesses growing slower (~15%), and are repositioning as "AI employees" around phone/text lead capture. AI receptionist is the fastest-emerging SMB AI category but mostly sold as low-ticket subscriptions or bundled by incumbents.

- Standalone reviews/messaging tools face compression as vertical SoRs and AI agents absorb those functions; the winning SMB wedge is increasingly "don't miss a lead" (answer every call/text, book the job) because the ROI is directly visible.

## 3. Outcome-based / usage-based pricing trends

### Takeaway
Customer-service AI has converged on per-resolution pricing ($0.99 Intercom Fin; ~$1.50-2 Zendesk; negotiated per-outcome at Sierra), while SMB receptionist tools still use flat monthly tiers or per-caller billing, and infrastructure (Retell/Vapi/Bland/ElevenLabs) charges per minute/usage. Hybrid (platform fee + outcomes) is becoming the norm.

### Cited Findings
- Intercom Fin: $0.99 per outcome, charged only when Fin resolves a conversation end-to-end; base plan $49/mo includes 50 resolutions — [Fin pricing](https://fin.ai/pricing); [Fin help center: Outcomes](https://fin.ai/help/en/articles/13975800-fin-pricing-outcomes); [Macha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/intercom-fin-pricing)
- Zendesk AI agents: ~$1.50 per automated resolution committed, ~$2 pay-as-you-go — [Macha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/intercom-fin-vs-zendesk-ai-pricing) [third-party]; [Intercom comparison](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison) [COMPETITOR SOURCE]
- Sierra: outcome-based, negotiated, unpublished — [Fin.ai](https://fin.ai/learn/sierra-ai-pricing) [COMPETITOR SOURCE]
- Salesforce Agentforce now runs three pricing models concurrently (per conversation, flex credits/per action, per-user) — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/salesforce-now-has-3-pricing-models-for-agentforce-and-maybe-right-now-thats-the-way-to-do-it)
- Per-conversation vs per-resolution: at a 60% resolution rate, "$1 per interaction" costs more than "$1 per resolution" — [Intercom comparison](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison)
- SMB AI receptionists: Goodcall bills by unique caller ($79-$249/mo); Rosie flat-rate unlimited minutes ($49-$299) — [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/best-ai-receptionist-for-small-business); [Allo](https://www.withallo.com/blog/ai-receptionist)
- Cursor's growth path (to >$4B) involved usage-based consumption beyond seat pricing — reported in [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/cursor-hit-2-billion-in-revenue-then-it-told-developers-to-stop-coding) [secondary]

### Inferences
- For SMB buyers, predictability beats pure usage: flat tiers and per-caller caps dominate the SMB receptionist market, while outcome pricing wins in mid-market/enterprise support. An SMB offer could mimic outcome pricing with "per booked appointment" or "per captured lead" plus a low base fee.
- Usage-based "ARR" inflates run-rate figures of AI-native companies; the report should caveat that these numbers are more volatile than seat-based ARR.

### Gaps
- No public per-minute pricing verified from Retell/Vapi/Bland primary pages in this pass.
- No data found on SMB-specific outcome pricing (e.g., pay per booked job) adoption rates.

## 4. Evidence of what SMB owners buy and keep (churn, ROI messaging)

### Takeaway
Retention is highest where software sits on money flows or the system of record (ServiceTitan NDR >110% for 3 years; Birdeye est. negative net churn), and lower in point communication tools (Weave GRR 90%, NRR 94%). ROI messaging across the category is framed as revenue captured (bigger tickets, more booked jobs, more sales), mostly vendor-claimed.

### Inferences
- SMBs keep tools that (1) touch revenue directly (payments, bookings, lead capture) and (2) become the system of record; they churn from standalone communication tools, especially in new verticals.
- The most persuasive ROI message for local businesses is "revenue recovered" (calls answered, jobs booked, ticket size), not time saved.
- Price increases can lift NRR temporarily (Weave) but gains fade — SMB price sensitivity is real.
