# Top US SaaS companies, all sectors: 2025-2026 benchmark

Research date: 2026-09-29. Most figures come from search-result extracts of company press releases and SEC filings (8-K, 10-K). Direct fetches of sec.gov, businesswire, yahoo finance and bullfincher were blocked by the network proxy, so numbers are as quoted in those search extracts. Where a source is a secondary aggregator, that is flagged.

Nationality flags. **Shopify** is Canadian (Ottawa). **Atlassian** began in Australia but now has a US domicile (Delaware, 2022). **monday.com** and **Wix** are Israeli (they file 6-K, not 10-K). **Oracle** and **Microsoft** are not pure SaaS, so only their cloud-application segments are relevant.

## 1. Top ~20 by revenue, with figures and dates (FY2025/FY2026)

### Takeaway
Microsoft (more than $331B total, with M365 Commercial cloud +17%) and Oracle ($67.4B total, about $16B in SaaS) are the giants, but they are mixed businesses. Among pure-play or SaaS-dominant US companies the ranking is: Salesforce ($41.5B), Adobe ($23.8B), ADP ($21.9B, payroll/HCM with a service-heavy mix), Intuit ($21.4B), ServiceNow (about $12.9B in subscription), Workday ($9.55B), Autodesk ($7.2B), Atlassian ($6.57B), Toast ($6.15B, mostly fintech/payments), GoDaddy ($4.95B), Zoom ($4.87B) and Snowflake (about $4.5B in product revenue). The highest growth is at Palantir (+56% in 2025, 2026 guidance about +61%), Snowflake (+29%), Datadog (+28%), Atlassian (+26%) and monday (+27%).

## 2. Which categories dominate

### Takeaway
By revenue, the biggest categories are productivity/collaboration (Microsoft 365, Atlassian, Zoom), CRM/front office (Salesforce, HubSpot), ERP/finance (Oracle, Workday Financials, NetSuite, Intuit QuickBooks), HR/payroll (ADP, Workday HCM, Paycom, Paylocity, Gusto) and creative/design (Adobe, Autodesk). The fastest-growing categories are data/AI platforms (Palantir, Snowflake, Datadog) and commerce/payments-embedded SaaS (Shopify, Toast).

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft's M365 growth came from ARPU (Copilot, E5) plus seats (+6%) from SMB and frontline workers — [Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)
- HR/payroll: ADP ($21.9B), Workday ($9.55B), Paycom ($2.05B) and Paylocity ($1.77B) together exceed $35B — sources in section 1.
- Data/AI: Palantir +56% and 139% NDR; Snowflake +29% and 125% NRR; Datadog +28% — sources in section 1.
- Salesforce bought Informatica (closed November 2025) to push into data management — [Salesforce 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108524/000110852426000060/crm-20260131.htm)
- Atlassian's reacceleration was driven by seat expansion plus cross-sell of Service Collection and Teamwork Collection and deployment of Rovo AI — [Atlassian Q4 FY26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)

### Inferences
- Horizontal back-office categories (payroll, accounting, ERP) show the steadiest revenue and the highest margins: ADP has 26.8% adjusted EBIT and Paycom has 43% adjusted EBITDA. Growth is modest (7-14%).
- Security, which the brief mentions, was not covered here. CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler and Fortinet are large US security players, but no 2025-26 figures were gathered. This is a gap.

### Gaps
- Security vendors (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Zscaler, Okta) were not researched.
- No Statista or Gartner category-share data was retrieved.

## 3. Which of these sell massively to SMBs (e.g. restaurants)

### Takeaway
The true SMB giants are Intuit (about 8.9M online paying customers across QuickBooks and Mailchimp), Wix (6.1M premium subscriptions), Square/Block (about 4M sellers), HubSpot (about 289K customers at about $11.7K average subscription revenue per customer), Shopify (millions of merchants, $378B GMV), Gusto (about 400K customers, unverified) and Toast (about 164K restaurant locations, $2.05B ARR). Their ARPU is low (hundreds to low thousands of dollars per year), so they monetize through payments, payroll and capital on top of the subscription.

## 4. Common success factors

### Takeaway
The leaders share four traits:
- **Strong expansion:** NRR above 120% at Palantir (139%), Snowflake (125%) and Datadog (about 120%).
- **Land-and-expand into multi-product platforms:** Datadog's customers above $100k make up 90% of ARR; Atlassian cross-sells Collections.
- **Product-led growth or freemium entry:** Atlassian, HubSpot, Zoom, monday.
- **Embedded payments and financial services at SMB scale:** payments are 76% of Shopify's revenue; Toast, Square and Intuit do the same.

Median public SaaS NRR has fallen to about 108% (from about 125% in 2022), so NRR above 110% is now a mark of a top-quartile company.

- Profitability now matters. Examples: ADP 26.8% adjusted EBIT margin, Paycom 43% adjusted EBITDA, Shopify 17% FCF margin, Datadog 26.7% FCF margin. Valuations hit a ten-year low (3.6x EV/NTM, February 2026), so the market rewards efficient growth — sources in section 1.

### Inferences
- A model built for SMBs such as restaurants should copy the SMB winners: a cheap or freemium subscription entry point, with ARPU expanded through payments, payroll, marketing and AI add-ons, not through seats. Pure subscription ARPU for SMB tools typically runs around $1K/year (Intuit) to $12K/year (HubSpot, Toast per location).
- High NRR (above 120%) mostly shows up in usage-priced enterprise data platforms. SMB-focused companies usually have NRR around or below 100-105% because of churn, and they rely on new-logo acquisition. This is an inference from general knowledge; SMB NRR figures were not verified in this research.
